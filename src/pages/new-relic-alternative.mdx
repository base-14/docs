# The New Relic alternative that doesn't charge per seat

_Last verified against published pricing on September 27, 2026._

> **TL;DR:** New Relic's user-based pricing charges per seat as well
> as per GB. A 100-engineer team with 100 K8s hosts pays
> ~$19,250/month on the Pro edition, even with a mix of Full
> Platform ($349), Core ($49) and Basic (free) users. base14 Scout:
> $250/month platform fee + $0.10/M metrics + $0.25/M logs & traces,
> no per-seat charges, no backend sampling, 30-day retention. Same
> infrastructure, ~$5,375/month. Built on OpenTelemetry.

A payments company went from 45 to 100 engineers. The New Relic bill
grew with it, and forced a decision about who gets full access and
who does not.

They ended up with 30 Full Platform users at $349/month, 25 Core
users at $49, and the rest on free Basic accounts.

The engineers on Basic can query data and build custom dashboards,
but can't open the curated APM, distributed tracing or
infrastructure views. During incidents, they rely on someone with a
Full Platform seat.

New Relic is a capable platform. The APM is solid, the UI is
polished, and NRQL is a powerful query language. The
[user-based pricing model](https://newrelic.com/pricing) is what
makes teams look elsewhere: every engineer who needs the full
product is another seat.

This page compares New Relic with
[base14 Scout](https://base14.io/scout) on cost at scale, data
retention, vendor lock-in and support.

## Why engineering teams look for a New Relic alternative

Teams rarely leave New Relic over product quality. They leave
because seat pricing ties the bill to headcount, which pushes teams
to limit who gets full access. What they want is
[unified observability](/blog/unified-observability) that everyone
on the team can use.

### The per-seat pricing problem

On user-based pricing, New Relic's cost has two axes: users and
data.

| User type     | Cost                      | Access                                                                                                   |
| :------------ | :------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Basic         | Free                      | Custom dashboards, queries, alerts and log search. No curated APM, infrastructure or tracing UIs         |
| Core          | $49/user/month            | Adds the Logs UI, Errors Inbox and CodeStream. Still no curated APM, infrastructure, K8s or tracing UIs  |
| Full Platform | $349/user/month (Pro)     | Everything: APM, distributed tracing, infrastructure, K8s, synthetics. $418.80 per user on monthly terms |
| Data ingest   | $0.40/GB, original option | After 100 GB free/month. Data Plus at $0.60/GB adds up to 90 days of retention and compliance features   |

The curated experiences teams use most during incidents - APM,
distributed tracing, infrastructure monitoring, Kubernetes - need a
Full Platform user at $349/user on Pro with an annual commitment.
Teams work around this with a tiered license structure: Full
Platform for SREs and on-call engineers, Core for developers who
need logs, and Basic for everyone else.

The workaround reduces cost but creates access gaps. An engineer
with Core access who needs to trace a failing request during an
incident can't open APM or distributed tracing. An engineer on
Basic can query the underlying data and build a custom dashboard,
but can't open the infrastructure or APM views.

New Relic also offers compute-based pricing on Pro and Enterprise,
billed in compute capacity units (CCUs) with no user licenses. It
removes the seat axis and replaces it with a charge for the queries
and features your team runs. Pricing is through New Relic sales.

**Want to see what observability costs without a seat tax?**
[Check base14's pricing](https://base14.io/pricing): $250/month platform
fee + $0.10/M metrics + $0.25/M logs & traces. You pay per telemetry
event, not per person.

### The Standard-to-Pro jump

New Relic's Standard edition allows up to 5 Full Platform users:
$10 for the first and $99 for each additional. A sixth Full Platform
user means moving to Pro, at $349 per user with an annual
commitment.

Five users on Standard cost $406/month. Six users on Pro cost
$2,094/month, about five times as much for one more person.

Teams at this size often keep 5 Full Platform users and put everyone
else on Core or Basic, with the access gaps described above.

### The retention trade-off

On the original data option, New Relic keeps APM events,
distributed traces and browser data for 8 days. Logs, raw
dimensional metrics and custom events keep 30 days, and aggregated
metrics keep 13 months.

With 8 days of traces and APM events, a latency regression that
started 10 days ago has no trace data left to explain it.

Data Plus, at $0.60/GB instead of $0.40/GB, adds up to 90 days of
retention for most data types, so APM data goes from 8 to 98 days.
It also adds FedRAMP Moderate and HIPAA eligibility, advanced log
obfuscation and enhanced data export. Retention can also be extended
as an add-on at $0.05/GB per 30 days added.

### The feature breadth problem

New Relic offers APM, Infrastructure, Logs, Browser, Mobile,
Synthetics, Serverless, Kubernetes monitoring, Network monitoring,
Vulnerability Management, CodeStream, Errors Inbox and more. The
breadth is real.

In the evaluations we run, most teams use APM, Logs and
Infrastructure. The platform's size has its own overhead: learning
NRQL, navigating nested UIs, and understanding which data sources
feed which dashboards. Regular users absorb that more easily than
occasional ones.

## What to look for in a New Relic alternative

If New Relic's pricing is too expensive for your team size, define
what "better" means before evaluating alternatives. Not every tool
solves the same problem.

A New Relic replacement should provide:

- **No per-seat pricing**: Your observability cost should scale with
  data, not headcount.
- **Unified signals**: Logs, metrics and traces in one query surface,
  not scattered across tabs.
- **Predictable pricing**: One cost axis, not two (seats + data).
- **[OpenTelemetry](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/) support**:
  Vendor-neutral instrumentation for portability.
- **30-day default retention across signals**: Including traces,
  which New Relic keeps for 8 days on the original data option.
- **Hands-on support**: Engineers who know your setup, not only a
  ticket queue.

### What using Scout is like

You open Scout, pick a service, and see logs, metrics and traces in
one view, without moving between APM, Logs and Infrastructure as
separate products.

The query language is standard SQL, not a proprietary DSL. Traces,
logs and metrics sit in tables such as `otel_traces`, `otel_logs`
and `otel_metrics_gauge`, with OpenTelemetry fields as columns. An
NRQL query like this one:

```sql
SELECT average(duration) FROM Transaction
WHERE appName = 'checkout-service' SINCE 1 hour ago
```

becomes this in Scout, with span duration stored in nanoseconds:

```sql
SELECT avg(Duration) / 1e6 AS avg_ms
FROM otel_traces
WHERE ServiceName = 'checkout-service'
  AND Timestamp >= now() - INTERVAL 1 HOUR
```

### Pricing: one axis instead of two

On user-based pricing, New Relic charges on two axes that scale
independently: per user and per GB of data ingested. Hiring
engineers raises the bill, and so does generating more telemetry.

Even with an optimized license mix (30 Full Platform, 25 Core, 45
Basic for a 100-person team), seats are the larger part of the bill:
~$11,700/month on seats and ~$7,550/month on data. Seats are 61% of
the total, with 70 of the 100 engineers on Core or Basic.

Scout's pricing has one axis:
[signals](/observability-cost-optimization). A signal is a single
telemetry event - a log line, a metric point, a trace span - and
you pay a $250/month platform fee plus per-signal rates:

- **Metrics**: $0.10 per million signals.
- **Logs & Trace spans**: $0.25 per million signals.

Adding engineers does not change the bill, and neither does adding
attributes such as stack traces to spans you already send. Sending
more spans, log records or metric points does.

### Estimating your signal count

A typical microservice generating structured JSON logs, basic
metrics and distributed traces produces roughly 50-100 million
signals per month. A 20-service backend with moderate traffic lands
between 1-2 billion signals/month total.

To convert your New Relic GB ingest to signal counts, assume about
1.3 KB per log line, about 0.8 KB per trace span and a few bytes per
metric point. Divide your monthly GB by those sizes to get signal
volume.

### Access: gated by license vs everyone sees the data

At an e-commerce company with 12 engineers and 5 Full Platform
users, a junior engineer on a Basic account couldn't open the APM or
tracing views during a checkout outage.

She asked the on-call lead to screen-share his view over Zoom while
she ran commands on her laptop.

The debugging session took far longer than it should have. Most of
the extra time was coordination: "scroll down," "click on that

[database query](https://base14.io/scout/pgx) much faster.

Seat limits also affect how engineers learn. Engineers who can't

When full access costs $349/month per person, teams tend to
concentrate it in a small group of licensed engineers, who then
field questions from everyone else.

Scout does not charge per seat. Every engineer, SRE and support
engineer who wants to understand system health gets full access.
The Startup tier covers up to 50 authorized users and Enterprise
raises the limit.

### Retention: 8 days for traces vs 30 days

With 8 days of APM events and traces, your team can't answer "was
this request slow last month?" from trace data. Logs keep 30 days on
New Relic's original data option, and aggregated metrics keep 13
months, which is longer than Scout's default.

Scout retains 30 days of full-resolution data for logs, metrics and
traces alike. Extended retention is available, without moving all
ingest to a higher per-GB rate.

### Data portability: proprietary vs OpenTelemetry-native

New Relic supports OpenTelemetry alongside its proprietary agents.
The ecosystem around it - NRQL queries, dashboard definitions, alert
configs - is still proprietary. Your instrumentation can be
portable while your dashboards, alerts and saved queries stay

Scout is built entirely on [OpenTelemetry](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/)

### Support: edition-based SLAs vs SRE partnership

New Relic's support SLA depends on the edition: a 2 business day

If your applications call LLM APIs - OpenAI, Anthropic, Bedrock, or

## Migration: from New Relic to base14 Scout

Switching from New Relic doesn't require re-instrumenting your
applications. New Relic already supports OpenTelemetry, and you run
both platforms for about a month before the cutover.

### Adopt or extend OpenTelemetry instrumentation

If you already send OTel data to New Relic, you have a head start.
If not, install [OpenTelemetry](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/) SDKs
alongside your existing New Relic agents. Both can run at once.

### Point the collector at Scout

Configure an
[OpenTelemetry Collector](/instrument/collector-setup/otel-collector-config)
to export to base14 Scout. Keep a copy flowing to New Relic during the
transition; the collector supports multiple exporters. For the
full setup, see
[building a production-ready OTel collector](/blog/production-ready-otel-collector).

### Run both in parallel

Run both platforms side by side for 2-4 weeks. Compare dashboards,
verify alert coverage, and make sure your team can answer the same
questions in Scout that they answer in New Relic today.

Our onboarding team works with your engineers during this period.

Dashboard recreation usually takes longer than collector setup:
expect 2-3 days on the collector and 1-2 weeks refining dashboards
and alerts. Our onboarding engineers do most of the work, but your
team knows what questions the dashboards need to answer, so plan
time for that conversation.

### Decommission the New Relic agents

Once your team is confident, remove the New Relic agents and cancel
the subscription. Your OTel instrumentation stays. If you ever want
to evaluate another backend, you change collector configuration,
not application code.

The whole process takes 4-6 weeks for a mid-size team. Our onboarding
assistance (first month free) covers the migration hands-on.
[Get started here](/getting-started).

## Who should switch (and who shouldn't)

### Switch to base14 Scout if

- **Your team is large and growing.** Past Standard's five Full
  Platform users, you decide who gets Full Platform, Core or Basic.
  Scout has no per-seat axis to manage.
- **Cost predictability matters.** Two cost axes (seats + data) make
  New Relic bills harder to forecast. One cost axis (signals) makes
  budgeting simpler.
- **You want open standards.** OTel-native instrumentation means your
  telemetry is portable, without proprietary agents or query
  languages.
- **You want every trace kept.** Scout does no backend sampling. New
  Relic's APM agents sample traces adaptively unless you use
  Infinite Tracing.
- **Longer trace retention matters.** Scout keeps 30 days for all
  signals, against 8 days for APM events and traces on New Relic's
  original data option, without a Data Plus premium.

### Stay on New Relic if

- **Your team has 5 or fewer engineers who need full access.** New
  Relic's Standard edition costs $10 for the first Full Platform
  user and $99/user for up to four more. The free tier (one Full
  Platform user, unlimited Basic users, 100 GB a month) covers solo
  developers.
- **Your team has deep NRQL expertise.** Scout uses standard SQL, which
  most engineers already know. But if your runbooks and on-call
  workflows have years of NRQL queries baked in, factor in the time to
  convert them. It's typically hours, not weeks, but it's real work.
- **You need long metric history at the default price.** New Relic
  keeps aggregated metrics for 13 months.
- **You'd rather pay for compute than for seats.** New Relic's
  compute-based pricing on Pro and Enterprise has no user licenses.

### The hybrid approach

OTel instrumentation can send data to multiple backends at once.
Many teams run New Relic and Scout in parallel during evaluation, so

### Do I need to learn a new query language to move from New Relic?

No. Scout uses standard SQL. This query lists the checkout
service's operations with spans slower than 500 ms (durations are
stored in nanoseconds):

```sql
SELECT SpanName, count(*) AS slow_spans
FROM otel_traces
WHERE ServiceName = 'checkout'
  AND Duration > 500000000
GROUP BY SpanName
```

NRQL queries translate clause by clause: `FROM Transaction` becomes
`FROM otel_traces`, `appName` becomes `ServiceName`, and `SINCE`
becomes a condition on `Timestamp`.
